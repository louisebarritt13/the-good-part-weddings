How Much Does an Intimate Wedding Actually Cost?

By Louise8 min readBudget & Costs · Planning Advice

The internet is full of "average wedding cost" figures that are not very useful. They tend to include venue hire for 150 people, catering for a hundred, entertainment for the whole evening. If you are planning a wedding for 20 or 30 people, those numbers do not apply to you in any meaningful way.

This article is a realistic cost breakdown specifically for intimate weddings: UK weddings with roughly 10 to 50 guests. The figures below come from actual supplier pricing. They will help you build a real budget rather than a notional one.

The honest overview first

A well-planned intimate wedding in the UK typically costs between £8,000 and £25,000. The range is wide because of three main variables: location (London and the South East cost more than most other regions), how many guests, and how much you spend on photography and venue.

At the lower end of that range, you are probably looking at a weekday registry office ceremony followed by a private restaurant dinner for 15 to 20 people, with mid-range photography. At the upper end, you might have a full weekend hire of a small country house venue for 40 to 50 guests, a photographer who specialises in documentary work, and excellent food and drink.

Some couples spend less. Some spend more. But if someone is quoting you significantly below £8,000 for a "real" wedding with guests, food and photography, something in that budget is not what it appears to be.

What actually drives the cost up

Photography

Photography is the most emotionally significant cost you will make and the one people most commonly underinvest in and later regret. A photographer who charges £1,200 and one who charges £2,800 are not delivering the same thing. For a small wedding, the photographer is often capturing your whole day: 20 to 30 people in intimate moments, candid details, the ceremony. Finding someone who is genuinely good at that, rather than formula venue photography, is worth the investment.

Venue

The venue cost is enormously variable. A restaurant private dining room for 25 guests might cost £800 to £1,500 in hire fees, with food and drink on top. A dedicated wedding venue for the same number might cost £3,000 to £5,000 for the space alone. The question is: what does the venue give you for that fee? If it comes with ceremony space, beautiful grounds and built-in flexibility, it may be worth it. If it is primarily the room itself, a good restaurant often delivers more for less.

London

Getting married in London carries a premium across almost every category. Venues, catering, photographers and florists all cost more in the capital than they do elsewhere in the UK. If budget matters and you are flexible on location, this is worth factoring into your planning early.

"The couples who feel best about what they spent are almost always the ones who identified the two or three things that mattered most to them, spent properly on those, and kept everything else proportionate."

Where you can spend less without compromising the day

Catering format

A seated dinner tends to cost more per head than a grazing table, shared platters or a relaxed buffet. If you love the idea of a beautiful spread of food that people help themselves to, this can deliver a genuinely good experience for less per head than a plated meal.

Florals

Flowers can eat budget quickly if you let them. Consider: what is the ceremony backdrop actually going to look like in photographs? A single architectural installation or a few well-chosen arrangements often photographs better than a lot of indifferent florals everywhere. Work with a florist who understands your aesthetic and can advise on where the investment matters most.

Weekdays

Many venues and suppliers offer lower rates for Monday to Thursday. If you have flexibility, a weekday can save you a meaningful amount. The tradeoff is that some guests may not be able to attend. For a small wedding, that is a real consideration.

Keep the guest list small

The single biggest lever on cost is the guest list. Each additional guest adds catering, potentially a chair and place setting, and accommodation if relevant. For couples who genuinely want something intimate, keeping the list tight does not feel like a compromise. It is the point.

Comparing intimate versus large wedding costs

The average UK wedding in 2026 costs around £20,000 to £27,000 (Bridebook). Many of those weddings have 100 or more guests. When you redistribute that budget across 25 people rather than 100, the per-person experience can be dramatically better. Better food. A photographer who actually has time to find the moments. A venue that fits the group.

This is why a lot of couples who plan intimate weddings intentionally come away feeling they got more for their money, even if they spent a similar total.

FAQs

Can I have a good intimate wedding for under £10,000?

Yes, but you need to be realistic about what that means. A weekday ceremony at a register office, a restaurant dinner for 15 to 20 people, and a mid-range photographer is achievable in that budget in most UK regions. In London it is tight. The key is knowing where to invest and where to save, and not trying to do everything on a compressed budget.

Should I include a contingency in my budget?

Yes. 10 to 15% is a sensible buffer. Things come up: a supplier you love costs more than you planned for, you decide you want additional flowers, the dress alterations cost more than expected. Having a buffer means these decisions do not become stressful.

Does an intimate wedding always save money compared to a bigger one?

Not necessarily. If you go small but choose a very high-end venue and premium suppliers, the total can rival a larger but more modest wedding. The saving comes from spending less per head on catering and fewer venue requirements, but that only plays out if you are not simultaneously upgrading everything else significantly.

What is not worth skimping on?

Photography. The day passes. The photos are what you have afterwards, and the gap between a good photographer and a bad one is enormous. Second: the food and drink experience. Food is what people remember. Everything else is relatively adjustable.
