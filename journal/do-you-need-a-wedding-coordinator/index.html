Do You Really Need an On-the-Day Wedding Coordinator?

By Louise 5 min read Planning Advice · Day Coordination

Honest answer: not every couple does. But the ones who don't have one? A surprising number of them tell me afterwards that they wish they had. Not because anything went badly wrong, but because they spent their wedding day managing things instead of actually being in it.

So let me give you the most useful version of this answer, which isn't "yes, hire a coordinator" (I'm biased, obviously). Here's a framework for actually deciding.

What does an on-the-day coordinator actually do?

The name is a bit misleading, because good on-the-day coordination actually starts weeks before the wedding. Here's what it typically covers:

  • Taking over supplier communication in the final 2 to 4 weeks, confirming timings, arrival windows, final details
  • Building the master schedule for the day and sharing it with every supplier involved
  • Being the single point of contact on the day, so no one is calling you or your parents with questions
  • Managing the timeline, keeping things moving without it feeling rushed
  • Solving problems quietly, before anyone notices them
  • Making sure you've eaten, had water, and had a moment with your partner
  • Overseeing the room flip if there is one
  • Being the last person in the venue at the end of the night

What it doesn't include: planning your wedding from scratch, finding your suppliers, designing your table arrangements, or anything that should have been decided months ago. If you need that, you need a fuller planning package rather than on-the-day support.

Who genuinely benefits from on-the-day coordination?

In my experience, it makes the most difference for:

Couples who've planned everything themselves

You've done all the hard work. You know exactly what you want. The last thing you want is to spend your wedding day being the person who knows all the answers. But that's exactly what happens if no one else is holding the schedule. An on-the-day coordinator means someone else carries that knowledge instead of you.

Couples with complex timelines

If your day involves multiple locations, a room flip, a live band and a DJ, a large catering operation, or any kind of complicated supplier choreography, the value of having one person managing all of it increases significantly.

Couples without a highly organised friend or family member to delegate to

Some couples do rely on a trusted friend or family member to manage the day. This can work, but it means that person is working, not celebrating. It also means they're managing things with an emotional investment in the outcome, which can make decision-making under pressure harder.

Couples who know they'll find it hard to switch off

Some people are just wired to notice when things are running 20 minutes late, or when the florist hasn't arrived yet. If that's you, knowing someone else is tracking everything can be genuinely freeing.

"The couples who get the most from on-the-day coordination aren't the ones with the most complicated weddings. They're the ones who have properly handed over responsibility. That takes real trust. It's worth it."

When might you not need one?

There are genuinely some situations where on-the-day coordination adds less value:

  • Your venue includes a dedicated coordinator. Some venues (particularly full-service hotels or dedicated wedding venues) provide their own day coordinator as part of the package. Check exactly what this covers, though. A venue coordinator manages the venue and its staff. They are not managing your personal timeline or your suppliers.
  • Your wedding is very small and simple. If you're getting married at a register office, going straight to a restaurant for dinner with 15 people, and you've booked one photographer. There's genuinely not much to coordinate. You could manage this yourselves.
  • You have a trusted, capable person who genuinely wants to take this on. If someone in your life is offering, is organised, and understands what they're volunteering for. This can work. Have a detailed handover conversation with them well before the day.

The question most people don't ask

When couples ask me whether they need a coordinator, they're usually asking: "is something going to go wrong without one?" That's the wrong question.

The better question is: "do I want to be the person responsible for making sure everything goes right on my wedding day?"

Because even if nothing goes wrong (and most of the time, most things don't), the experience of being the person who might need to handle something is completely different from being a guest at your own wedding.

The couples I work with who've had the most genuinely relaxed, fully present wedding days are the ones who handed off responsibility properly. They didn't spend the morning anxiously refreshing supplier emails. They trusted the schedule was being held by someone else. And they just got married.

FAQs

What's the difference between an on-the-day coordinator and a wedding planner?

A wedding planner works with you throughout the planning process: finding suppliers, managing budgets, designing your concept, making decisions alongside you. An on-the-day coordinator steps in at the end of the planning process and manages the execution of everything you've already planned. Some planners offer both; on-the-day-only packages are for couples who've planned everything themselves.

How much does on-the-day wedding coordination cost in the UK?

Fees vary widely. As a rough guide, independent coordinators in the UK typically charge between £600 and £1,800 for on-the-day support, depending on the scope, location and experience level. This usually includes a handover meeting, supplier communication in the final weeks, and full management on the day itself.

Can my venue coordinator manage everything?

A venue coordinator manages the venue, including the catering team, the room setup, the venue staff. They are not usually responsible for managing your personal photographer, your band, your florist, your transport, your timeline or your guests. If you want someone managing all of those things, you need a separate coordinator.

When should I book an on-the-day coordinator?

As early as you can, ideally at least 6 months before your wedding. Good coordinators book up, and you want time for a proper handover. Booking early also means you have someone to call if questions come up during planning.
