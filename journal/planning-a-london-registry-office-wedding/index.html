Planning a London Registry Office Wedding: Everything You Actually Need to Know

By Louise8 min readLondon & Registry · Planning Advice

Registry office weddings in London are having a moment. And honestly, it is about time. For years they were treated as the budget option, the compromise, or the thing you did before the "real" wedding abroad. But more and more couples are choosing them deliberately. Not because they cannot afford anything else, but because a small, meaningful ceremony in a beautiful room with the people who matter most is exactly what they want.

If that sounds like you, this guide is for you. Here is everything you need to know about booking, costs, making it feel personal and planning a great day around it.

What is a registry office wedding?

A registry office wedding (or register office wedding, both terms are used) is a civil ceremony legally conducted by a registrar from your local council. It is a fully legal marriage, with no religious content permitted by law. The ceremony itself is usually 15 to 25 minutes long.

In London, this means getting married at one of the capital's many register offices. From the elegant surroundings of Chelsea Old Town Hall to the grand rooms at Islington Town Hall or the intimate ceremony rooms at Hackney and Greenwich. Many London register offices have genuinely beautiful approved venues attached, or ceremony rooms that photograph extremely well.

How the booking process works

Step 1: Give notice of marriage

Before anything else, both of you need to give notice of marriage at the register office in the borough where you each live. This must happen at least 29 days before your wedding, and there is often a waiting list just to give notice, so factor in extra time. If you live in different boroughs, you each give notice separately at your own local office.

At the notice appointment, bring proof of name, age, nationality and address. The list of accepted documents is on your council's website. Check it in advance, as missing paperwork means they will not see you.

Step 2: Book your ceremony

Separately, you book the ceremony itself at the register office (or approved venue) where you want to get married. This does not have to be in the borough where either of you lives.

London's most popular register offices book up fast. Marylebone, Chelsea and Islington in particular have waiting lists of 12 months or more for Saturdays. If you have a specific venue or date in mind, treat this booking exactly like a traditional wedding venue and prioritise it.

"The number one thing I tell couples planning a London registry wedding: do not assume it will be easy to book. Have a backup borough in mind and be flexible on dates."

Step 3: Plan your day around it

Once your ceremony is booked, the rest of the day is entirely up to you. Most couples head to a private dining room at a restaurant, a private members club, or a small venue for a reception or dinner afterwards.

What does a London registry office wedding actually cost?

The ceremony fee covers room hire and your registrars. Then everything built around it, for a wedding with 20 to 50 guests:

Most couples planning a London registry wedding with a proper celebration afterwards spend somewhere between £5,000 and £15,000 in total, depending on guest numbers, venue choice and how much they invest in photography and food. The ceremony itself is a small fraction of that. The flexibility is entirely in your hands.

Compare that with the average London wedding cost of around £27,000 (Bridebook, 2026) and it becomes clear why registry weddings appeal to couples who want a genuinely good day without spending money on things they do not care about.

Can you make a registry office wedding feel special?

Yes. Completely. And this is what I spend most time talking to couples about, because there is sometimes an assumption that a civil ceremony has to be functional and plain. It does not.

Choose the right room

London has extraordinary register offices. Some have rooms that feel genuinely grand, with high ceilings, tall windows and beautiful period detail. Research the ceremony rooms before you decide where to book. Instagram and council websites both have photos.

Personalise your ceremony

Civil ceremonies allow you to personalise the wording within certain legal limits. You can add readings, choose music and write personal vows to exchange alongside the legal declarations. Talk to your registrar about what is possible. They are usually very helpful and want the ceremony to feel meaningful.

Invest in the details around it

Flowers, buttonholes, a beautiful outfit, a photographer who understands small weddings. These are what make a registry office wedding feel like a wedding. It is not about scale. It is about intentionality.

Photography at a registry office wedding

Photography is the most important thing to get right. Because you will not have venue grounds, a church door or a countryside backdrop, you are relying on your photographer to find the story in smaller, quieter moments.

Look for photographers who specifically mention documentary or reportage work, and whose portfolios include small weddings or elopements. Ask to see a full gallery from a small wedding, not just highlights. You want to see how they handle an intimate room, natural light and genuine emotion.

What to do after the ceremony

This is completely open. Some ideas that work well:

  • A private dining room at a restaurant you love (many restaurants offer this for groups of 10 to 50)
  • A drinks reception followed by dinner at a hotel or private members club
  • A hired space for a relaxed evening party with a wider circle
  • An afternoon drinks reception, then dinner for the closest guests only

The most common structure I see: ceremony with 20 to 40 guests, followed by a long, relaxed lunch or dinner at a restaurant that feels special to you. No formal schedule. Just good food, the right people and the best day.

FAQs

How far in advance do I need to book?

For popular boroughs like Marylebone, Chelsea and Islington, book as far ahead as possible. 12 to 18 months for a Saturday is not unusual. Weekday ceremonies have better availability. You need to give notice at least 29 days before the ceremony, but notice appointments themselves often have a waiting list too.

Can we have music at a registry office ceremony?

Yes. You can play recorded music as guests arrive, as you walk in and as you leave. Live music may be possible depending on the venue. All music must be non-religious.

Can we have a reading?

Yes, readings are welcome. They must be non-religious, but there are many beautiful secular poems and prose pieces that work brilliantly. Your registrar will usually need to see these in advance.

What is the difference between a register office and an approved venue?

A register office is the council's own building. An approved venue is a separate licensed location such as a hotel or historic house, where registrars attend to conduct the ceremony. Approved venues often have more atmosphere and flexibility, but carry higher hire fees.

Do we need a wedding planner?

Not necessarily. The ceremony is managed by your registrar. If you are keeping things simple, you can usually manage it yourselves. If you want help with logistics, suppliers or coordinating a larger celebration around the ceremony, that is where I can help. This article might help you decide.
